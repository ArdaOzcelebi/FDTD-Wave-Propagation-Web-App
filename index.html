How FDTD Works

Four pillars behind the algorithm — from raw Maxwell to a computable algorithm.

01
∇×

Maxwell's Curl Equations

FDTD attacks the raw, time-dependent curl equations directly — no Green's functions, no integral transforms.

$$\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\mu_0 \frac{\partial \mathbf{H}}{\partial t}$$ $$\nabla \times \mathbf{H} = \epsilon \frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t} + \sigma \mathbf{E}$$

The beauty is the symmetry: a changing H field creates a spatial curl in E, and vice versa. FDTD exploits this looping relationship to simulate waves forward in time.

02
⬡

The Yee Cell (1966)

Kane Yee's genius: stagger E on cube edges and H on cube faces — offset by half a spatial step. This perfectly mirrors the curl operator.

E field — on edges
Ex — x-edges
Ey — y-edges
Ez — z-edges
H field — on faces
Hx — YZ faces
Hy — XZ faces
Hz — XY faces

Drag the diagram to orbit. The staggered placement means the curl of E is computed using four surrounding E values — a simple algebraic difference, no integrals required.

⏱

Leapfrog Integration

Fields are also staggered in time by half a step Δt/2, creating the leapfrog algorithm.

En
Hn+½
En+1
HUse En → advance H by ½Δt
EUse Hn+½ → advance E by ½Δt
∞Repeat — the wave self-propagates!
$$H^{n+\frac{1}{2}} = H^{n-\frac{1}{2}} - \frac{\Delta t}{\mu_0} \nabla \times E^n$$ $$E^{n+1} = C_a E^n + C_b \nabla \times H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}$$

⚖

Strengths & Limitations

No method is perfect — knowing the trade-offs is part of engineering judgment.

✓ Strengths
  • Broadband in one shot — inject a Gaussian pulse, take a Fourier transform, get the full spectrum.
  • Arbitrary materials — assign different ε and μ to every single cell (biological tissues, composites, metamaterials).
  • Intuitive — you literally watch the wave propagate in time.
  • Explicit — no matrix inversions, scales to massive grids.
✗ Limitations
  • Staircasing — curved surfaces are approximated by jagged staircase steps on the cubic grid.
  • Dispersion — waves traveling diagonally through the grid travel at a slightly different speed (numerical artifact).
  • Courant limit — time step is constrained: $\Delta t \leq \frac{\Delta x}{c\sqrt{3}}$.
  • Memory — 3D grids with fine resolution consume vast RAM.

💡

Why is this so powerful?

A frequency-domain method (like MoM or FEM) must run a separate simulation at every single frequency. FDTD runs once with a broadband pulse and extracts data for all frequencies simultaneously via Fourier Transform. This makes it the industry standard for antenna bandwidth analysis and bioEM studies.

Free Space Propagation

A Ricker-wavelet point source in vacuum. Watch the spherical electromagnetic wave expand outward at the speed of light, absorbed at the PML boundaries. Confirm the wave speed by timing the arrival at different distances.

  • Observe spherical wavefronts expanding at c
  • Note the PML boundaries absorbing outgoing waves
  • Observe the Ricker wavelet waveform at the probe

02

Double-Slit Diffraction

A plane wave hits a conducting wall with two narrow slits. The wave bends around each slit (diffraction) and the two daughter waves interfere on the far side — bright fringes where they add, dark fringes where they cancel.

  • Identify the central bright fringe and side lobes
  • Observe Huygens wavelets emerging from each slit
  • Compare fringe spacing to λ·L/d formula
🔷
03

Dielectric Interface (Snell's Law)

A plane wave hits a dielectric slab (εᵣ = 4, n = 2). Part of the wave reflects at the interface (obeying Fresnel equations) and part transmits — slowing to c/n inside the denser medium. The wavelength shortens inside.

  • Measure reflected vs transmitted amplitude
  • Observe the wavelength halving inside the slab
  • Tune εᵣ and watch reflection coefficient change
📦
04

Resonant Cavity Modes

A short electromagnetic pulse is injected inside a PEC (Perfect Electric Conductor) box. The pulse rings down into the cavity's natural resonant modes — standing waves with frequencies f = c/(2L)·√(p²+q²+r²) where p,q,r are integers.

  • Watch the initial pulse split into standing waves
  • Compute the expected resonance frequency
  • Use the FFT panel to identify mode frequencies
📡
05

Rectangular Waveguide

A metallic rectangular waveguide confines and guides electromagnetic energy. Below the cut-off frequency the wave is evanescent and decays exponentially. Above cut-off, discrete TE₁₀, TE₂₀… modes propagate.

  • Observe mode confinement between the metal walls
  • Identify the field null planes inside the guide
  • Lower the frequency and observe evanescent decay
⚡
06

Radiating Dipole Antenna

A short current element (Hertzian dipole) radiates an electromagnetic wave. The characteristic toroidal radiation pattern emerges: maximum in the equatorial plane, zero along the dipole axis — the classic antenna pattern.

  • Observe the near-field → far-field transition
  • Identify the null along the z-axis (dipole axis)
  • Measure the donut-shaped radiation pattern

Background

About FDTD & This Simulator

📖 A Brief History

In 1966, Kane Yee published a landmark paper in IEEE Transactions on Antennas and Propagation introducing the staggered-grid finite-difference scheme for solving Maxwell's equations in the time domain. His key insight — offsetting E and H fields in both space and time — produced a naturally stable, second-order-accurate algorithm.

For two decades FDTD was primarily a research curiosity. In the 1980s and 1990s, as computers became powerful enough to handle meaningful grid sizes, FDTD exploded into commercial use. Today it is the dominant computational method for antenna design, photonic devices, bioelectromagnetics, and radar cross-section analysis.

"The Yee cell is one of the most elegant data structures in all of numerical physics."

🛠 This Simulator

Built entirely in vanilla JavaScript + Three.js — no server, no native libraries. The FDTD engine runs in a Web Worker thread so the UI stays responsive even during heavy computation. Field data is transferred to the rendering thread via zero-copy ArrayBuffer transfer (postMessage with Transferable ownership).

  • Grid: 60³ – 100³ Yee cells (configurable)
  • Fields: All six components Ex, Ey, Ez, Hx, Hy, Hz
  • Boundaries: Graded-conductivity PML (10 cells)
  • Visualization: Three.js DataTexture slice planes, ShaderMaterial colormap
  • Sources: Point, plane-wave, dipole; Ricker / sinusoidal / Gaussian waveforms
